Why I Switched From Being a Software Engineer to AI Engineer

August 17, 2026By Safiullah Korai8 min read

Let me clear up the title first: I am not abandoning Software Engineering. Not even close. I am moving toward AI Engineering because I want to build on the engineering foundation I already have and learn how to build software that can work with data, models, and intelligent systems.

For a while, I kept describing my future in a fairly simple way: I wanted to become a Software Engineer. That still describes me. I still love building applications, solving problems, thinking about architecture, working with APIs, learning frameworks, and turning an idea into something people can actually use.

But as I kept learning, another question became harder to ignore:

What happens when the software itself can learn, reason over information, generate outputs, and adapt to the problem it is solving?

That question pulled me deeper into Artificial Intelligence. And eventually, I realized that AI Engineering was not a completely different road. For me, it was the next layer of the same road.

1. Software Engineering gave me the foundation

I don't think I could explain my interest in AI Engineering without first talking about why Software Engineering matters so much to me.

Software Engineering taught me to think in systems. It taught me that writing code is only one part of building software. You have to understand requirements, structure, maintainability, testing, APIs, databases, deployment, users, failures, and all the little decisions that turn a collection of files into an actual product.

My journey has included different languages, web development, mobile development, APIs, databases, GitHub, Linux, cloud technologies, and especially Flutter. Every project has added another piece to the way I think about engineering.

That foundation is not something I want to throw away just because AI is growing rapidly.

In fact, it is one of the main reasons I want to learn AI properly.

2. AI made me curious about what comes next

As I started paying more attention to AI, I noticed that I was not only interested in using AI tools. I wanted to understand what was underneath them.

How do models learn from data? What makes a neural network useful? How does a computer vision system recognize patterns in an image? How do large language models process information? How do you evaluate a model instead of simply trusting its output? And how do you turn a model into a reliable part of a real application?

Those questions felt familiar because they were still engineering questions.

The difference was that the systems I wanted to build increasingly involved machine learning and intelligent behavior.

That is where AI Engineering became interesting to me.

3. I don't want to be only an AI tool user

There is a huge difference between using AI and understanding enough to engineer with it.

I use AI tools. Like many developers, they have become part of my workflow for brainstorming, learning, debugging, exploring ideas, and accelerating repetitive work. But I don't want my understanding of AI to stop at prompting a model and copying its output.

I want to know what I am building.

That means going back to fundamentals: Python, mathematics, data structures, statistics, machine learning, deep learning, model evaluation, APIs, deployment, and the software engineering practices needed to make AI systems reliable.

It is a much longer journey than simply learning a new framework. And honestly, that is part of what makes it exciting.

4. The intersection is more interesting than either label

The more I think about my career, the less useful the idea of choosing only one label becomes.

A modern AI product still needs software.

A machine learning model still needs data pipelines, APIs, infrastructure, interfaces, authentication, storage, monitoring, testing, and deployment. Someone still has to turn a model into a product that works for real users.

That intersection is exactly where I want to grow.

Software Engineering → AI Engineering
Not a replacement. Not a reset. An expansion of what I can build.

I want to be able to build the application and understand the intelligence inside it.

5. My Flutter journey is still part of this

One thing I want to make especially clear is that moving toward AI Engineering does not mean I suddenly stopped caring about Flutter.

Flutter has been a major part of my recent learning. Building mobile applications has forced me to think about state management, architecture, APIs, local storage, authentication, user experience, debugging, and the realities of shipping software.

Those skills remain useful.

In the future, I can imagine using Flutter to build the interface for an AI-powered product while Python and machine learning systems handle the intelligence behind it. The technologies can work together instead of competing for space on my roadmap.

So yes, I am still learning Flutter. I am still building software. I am still a Software Engineer.

6. My next step is Python and machine learning

The next stage of my learning is becoming more deliberate.

I want to strengthen my Python skills first, then move deeper into NumPy, Pandas, machine learning concepts, PyTorch, computer vision, and eventually the tools and architectures used in real AI systems.

I am particularly interested in medical AI and explainable AI because my academic work has already exposed me to that direction. I want to eventually move from writing about ideas and research to actually implementing them and understanding the engineering behind them.

That means there will be plenty of difficult days ahead. There will be mathematics I have to relearn, models that won't work, bugs I won't immediately understand, papers that take multiple readings, and projects that fail.

That's okay.

I'm not looking for the shortest route. I'm trying to build a strong one.

7. I am not changing who I am as an engineer

This is probably the most important part of this article.

I don't want to wake up one day and say, “I'm an AI Engineer, therefore everything I learned in Software Engineering doesn't matter anymore.”

That would be backwards.

Good AI engineering still requires good engineering.

I want to keep improving at writing maintainable code, designing systems, understanding users, building products, working with teams, documenting what I learn, and shipping things. I simply want to add another layer of technical depth to that foundation.

If anything, becoming stronger in AI should make me a better Software Engineer, because it will force me to understand new types of systems and new ways of thinking about software.

8. So, why the switch?

Because I want to build more.

Because I am curious about intelligent systems.

Because AI is changing how software is created and how software itself behaves.

Because I don't want to watch that transformation from the sidelines.

And because I think my Software Engineering foundation gives me a strong place to start.

I am not leaving Software Engineering behind. I am carrying it with me.

The goal isn't to become “less of a Software Engineer” and “more of an AI Engineer.”
The goal is to become an engineer who can build software and understand the intelligence that powers it.

Where I am heading now

My roadmap is not about rushing to put “AI Engineer” on a profile headline and calling the journey complete.

It is about earning that title through learning and building.

  • Strengthen Python and core programming fundamentals.
  • Learn the mathematics and statistics needed for machine learning.
  • Build practical machine learning and deep learning projects.
  • Explore computer vision and explainable AI.
  • Learn how models become reliable, usable software systems.
  • Keep building with Software Engineering and Flutter.
  • Write about what I learn so I can explain it clearly.

There is a lot ahead. And that's exactly why I am excited.

Software Engineer today. AI Engineer in progress.

Not a farewell to Software Engineering. Just the next chapter.

— Safiullah Korai
